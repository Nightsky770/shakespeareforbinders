\Scene{2}[\character{Olivia's} house.]

\enter{Maria, Feste}

\speech[Maria]{Nay, I prithee, put on this gown and this beard; make him believe thou art Sir Topas the curate: do it quickly; I'll call Sir Toby the whilst.}

\exit{Maria}

\speech[Feste]{Well, I'll put it on, and I will dissemble myself in't; and I would I were the first that ever dissembled in such a gown. 
\stage{He puts on gown and beard.}
I am not tall enough to become the function well, nor lean enough to be thought a good student; but to be said an honest man and a good housekeeper goes as fairly as to say a careful man and a great scholar. The competitors enter.}

\enter{Sir Toby Belch, Maria}

\speech[Toby]{Jove bless thee, master Parson.}

\speech[Feste]{\textit{Bonos dies,} Sir Toby: for, as the old hermit of Prague, that never saw pen and ink, very wittily said to a niece of King Gorboduc, <That that is is;> so I, being Master Parson, am Master Parson; for, what is <that> but <that,> and <is> but <is>?}

\speech[Toby]{To him, Sir Topas.}

\speech[Feste]{\stage{Disguising his voice} What, ho, I say! peace in this prison!}

\speech[Toby]{The knave counterfeits well; a good knave.}

\speech[Malvolio]{\stage{Within} Who calls there?}

\speech[Feste]{Sir Topas the curate, who comes to visit Malvolio the lunatic.}

\speech[Malvolio]{Sir Topas, Sir Topas, good Sir Topas, go to my lady.}

\speech[Feste]{Out, hyperbolical fiend! how vexest thou this man! talkest thou nothing but of ladies?}

\speech[Toby]{\aside{Well said, Master Parson.}}

\speech[Malvolio]{Sir Topas, never was man thus wronged: good Sir Topas, do not think I am mad: they have laid me here in hideous darkness.}

\speech[Feste]{Fie, thou dishonest Satan! I call thee by the most modest terms; for I am one of those gentle ones that will use the devil himself with courtesy: sayest thou that house is dark?}


\speech[Malvolio]{As hell, Sir Topas.}

\speech[Feste]{Why it hath bay windows transparent as barricadoes, and the clerestories toward the south-north are as lustrous as ebony; and yet complainest thou of obstruction?}

\speech[Malvolio]{I am not mad, Sir Topas: I say to you, this house is dark.}

\speech[Feste]{Madman, thou errest: I say, there is no darkness but ignorance; in which thou art more puzzled than the Egyptians in their fog.}

\speech[Malvolio]{I say, this house is as dark as ignorance, though ignorance were as dark as hell; and I say, there was never man thus abused. I am no more mad than you are: make the trial of it in any constant question.}

\speech[Feste]{What is the opinion of Pythagoras concerning wild fowl?}

\speech[Malvolio]{That the soul of our grandam might haply inhabit a bird.}

\speech[Feste]{What thinkest thou of his opinion?}

\speech[Malvolio]{I think nobly of the soul, and no way approve his opinion.}

\speech[Feste]{Fare thee well. Remain thou still in darkness: thou shalt hold the opinion of Pythagoras ere I will allow of thy wits, and fear to kill a woodcock, lest thou dispossess the soul of thy grandam. Fare thee well.}

\speech[Malvolio]{Sir Topas, Sir Topas!}

\speech[Toby]{My most exquisite Sir Topas!}

\speech[Feste]{Nay, I am for all waters.}

\speech[Maria]{Thou mightst have done this without thy beard and gown: he sees thee not.}

\speech[Toby]{To him in thine own voice, and bring me word how thou findest him: I would we were well rid of this knavery. If he may be conveniently delivered, I would he were, for I am now so far in offence with my niece that I cannot pursue with any safety this sport to the upshot. Come by and by to my chamber.}

\exit{Sir Toby Belch, Maria}

\end{verse}

\speech[Malvolio]{Fool!}

\speech[Feste]{

\speech[Malvolio]{Fool!}

\speech[Feste]{

\speech[Malvolio]{Fool, I say!}

\speech[Feste]{
\begin{verse}\itshape

Who calls, ha?}

\speech[Malvolio]{Good fool, as ever thou wilt deserve well at my hand, help me to a candle, and pen, ink and paper: as I am a gentleman, I will live to be thankful to thee for't.}

\speech[Feste]{Master Malvolio?}

\speech[Malvolio]{Ay, good fool.}

\speech[Feste]{Alas, sir, how fell you besides your five wits?}

\speech[Malvolio]{Fool, there was never a man so notoriously abused: I am as well in my wits, fool, as thou art.}

\speech[Feste]{But as well? then you are mad indeed, if you be no better in your wits than a fool.}

\speech[Malvolio]{They have here propertied me; keep me in darkness, send ministers to me, asses, and do all they can to face me out of my wits.}

\speech[Feste]{Advise you what you say; the minister is here. \stage{In the voice of \character{Sir Topas}.} Malvolio, Malvolio, thy wits the heavens restore! endeavour thyself to sleep, and leave thy vain bibble babble.}

\speech[Malvolio]{Sir Topas!}

\speech[Feste]{
\stage{as \character{Sir Topas}}
Maintain no words with him, good fellow. 

\stage{as himself}
Who, I, sir? not I, sir. God be wi' you, good Sir Topas. 

\stage{as \character{Sir Topas}}
Marry, amen. 

\stage{as himself}
I will, sir, I will.}

\speech[Malvolio]{Fool, fool, fool, I say!}

\speech[Feste]{Alas, sir, be patient. What say you sir? I am shent for speaking to you.}

\speech[Malvolio]{Good fool, help me to some light and some paper: I tell thee, I am as well in my wits as any man in Illyria.}

\speech[Feste]{Well-a-day that you were, sir}

\speech[Malvolio]{By this hand, I am. Good fool, some ink, paper and light; and convey what I will set down to my lady: it shall advantage thee more than ever the bearing of letter did.}

\speech[Feste]{I will help you to't. But tell me true, are you not mad indeed? or do you but counterfeit?}

\speech[Malvolio]{Believe me, I am not; I tell thee true.}

\speech[Feste]{Nay, I'll ne'er believe a madman till I see his brains. I will fetch you light and paper and ink.}

\speech[Malvolio]{Fool, I'll requite it in the highest degree: I prithee, be gone.}
