\Scene{5}[\character{Olivia's} house]

\enter{Maria, Feste}

\speech[Maria]{Nay, either tell me where thou hast been, or I will not open my lips so wide as a bristle may enter in way of thy excuse: my lady will hang thee for thy absence.}

\speech[Feste]{Let her hang me: he that is well hanged in this world needs to fear no colours.}

\speech[Maria]{Make that good.}

\speech[Feste]{He shall see none to fear.}

\speech[Maria]{A good lenten answer: I can tell thee where that saying was born, of <I fear no colours.>}

\speech[Feste]{Where, good Mistress Mary?}

\speech[Maria]{In the wars; and that may you be bold to say in your foolery.}

\speech[Feste]{Well, God give them wisdom that have it; and those that are fools, let them use their talents.}

\speech[Maria]{Yet you will be hanged for being so long absent; or, to be turned away, is not that as good as a hanging to you?}

\speech[Feste]{Many a good hanging prevents a bad marriage; and, for turning away, let summer bear it out.}

\speech[Maria]{You are resolute, then?}

\speech[Feste]{Not so, neither; but I am resolved on two points.}

\speech[Maria]{That if one break, the other will hold; or, if both break, your gaskins fall.}

\speech[Feste]{Apt, in good faith; very apt. Well, go thy way; if Sir Toby would leave drinking, thou wert as witty a piece of Eve's flesh as any in Illyria.}

\speech[Maria]{Peace, you rogue, no more o' that. Here comes my lady: make your excuse wisely, you were best.}

\exit{Maria}

\speech[Feste]{Wit, an't be thy will, put me into good fooling! Those wits, that think they have thee, do very oft prove fools; and I, that am sure I lack thee, may pass for a wise man: for what says Quinapalus? <Better a witty fool, than a foolish wit.> 

\enter*{\character{Olivia} with \character{Malvolio}}

God bless thee, lady!}


\speech[Olivia]{Take the fool away.}

\speech[Feste]{Do you not hear, fellows? Take away the lady.}

\speech[Olivia]{Go to, you're a dry fool; I'll no more of you: besides, you grow dishonest.}

\speech[Feste]{Two faults, madonna, that drink and good counsel will amend: for give the dry fool drink, then is the fool not dry: bid the dishonest man mend himself; if he mend, he is no longer dishonest; if he cannot, let the botcher mend him. Any thing that's mended is but patched: virtue that transgresses is but patched with sin; and sin that amends is but patched with virtue. If that this simple syllogism will serve, so; if it will not, what remedy? As there is no true cuckold but calamity, so beauty's a flower. The lady bade take away the fool; therefore, I say again, take her away.}

\speech[Olivia]{Sir, I bade them take away you.}

\speech[Feste]{Misprision in the highest degree! Lady, cucullus non facit monachum; that's as much to say as I wear not motley in my brain. Good madonna, give me leave to prove you a fool.}

\speech[Olivia]{Can you do it?}

\speech[Feste]{Dexterously, good madonna.}

\speech[Olivia]{Make your proof.}

\speech[Feste]{I must catechise you for it, madonna: good my mouse of virtue, answer me.}

\speech[Olivia]{Well, sir, for want of other idleness, I'll bide your proof.}

\speech[Feste]{Good madonna, why mournest thou?}

\speech[Olivia]{Good fool, for my brother's death.}

\speech[Feste]{I think his soul is in hell, madonna.}

\speech[Olivia]{I know his soul is in heaven, fool.}

\speech[Feste]{The more fool, madonna, to mourn for your brother's soul being in heaven. Take away the fool, gentlemen.}

\speech[Olivia]{What think you of this fool, Malvolio? doth he not mend?}

\speech[Malvolio]{Yes, and shall do till the pangs of death shake him: infirmity, that decays the wise, doth ever make the better fool.}

\speech[Feste]{God send you, sir, a speedy infirmity, for the better increasing your folly! Sir Toby will be sworn that I am no fox; but he will not pass his word for two pence that you are no fool.}

\speech[Olivia]{How say you to that, Malvolio?}

\speech[Malvolio]{I marvel your ladyship takes delight in such a barren rascal: I saw him put down the other day with an ordinary fool that has no more brain than a stone. Look you now, he's out of his guard already; unless you laugh and minister occasion to him, he is gagged. I protest, I take these wise men, that crow so at these set kind of fools, no better than the fools' zanies.}

\speech[Olivia]{Oh, you are sick of self-love, Malvolio, and taste with a distempered appetite. To be generous, guiltless and of free disposition, is to take those things for bird-bolts that you deem cannon-bullets: there is no slander in an allowed fool, though he do nothing but rail; nor no railing in a known discreet man, though he do nothing but reprove.}

\speech[Feste]{Now Mercury endue thee with leasing, for thou speakest well of fools!}

\stage{Re-enter \character{Maria}}


\speech[Maria]{Madam, there is at the gate a young gentleman much desires to speak with you.}

\speech[Olivia]{From the Count Orsino, is it?}

\speech[Maria]{I know not, madam: 'tis a fair young man, and well attended.}

\speech[Olivia]{Who of my people hold him in delay?}

\speech[Maria]{Sir Toby, madam, your kinsman.}

\speech[Olivia]{Fetch him off, I pray you; he speaks nothing but madman: fie on him!

\exit{Maria}

Go you, Malvolio: if it be a suit from the count, I am sick, or not at home; what you will, to dismiss it.

\exit{Malvolio}

Now you see, sir, how your fooling grows old, and people dislike it.}

\speech[Feste]{Thou hast spoke for us, madonna, as if thy eldest son should be a fool; whose skull Jove cram with brains! for,—here he comes,—one of thy kin has a most weak pia mater.}

\enter{Sir Toby Belch}

\speech[Olivia]{By mine honour, half drunk. What is he at the gate, cousin?}

\speech[Toby]{A gentleman.}

\speech[Olivia]{A gentleman! what gentleman?}

\speech[Toby]{'Tis a gentle man here—a plague o' these pickle-herring! How now, sot!}

\speech[Feste]{Good Sir Toby!}

\speech[Olivia]{Cousin, cousin, how have you come so early by this lethargy?}

\speech[Toby]{Lechery! I defy lechery. There's one at the gate.}

\speech[Olivia]{Ay, marry, what is he?}

\speech[Toby]{Let him be the devil, an he will, I care not: give me faith, say I. Well, it's all one.}
\exit{Toby}

\speech[Olivia]{What's a drunken man like, fool?}

\speech[Feste]{Like a drowned man, a fool and a mad man: one draught above heat makes him a fool; the second mads him; and a third drowns him.}

\speech[Olivia]{Go thou and seek the crowner, and let him sit o' my coz; for he's in the third degree of drink, he's drowned: go, look after him.}

\speech[Feste]{He is but mad yet, madonna; and the fool shall look to the madman.}

\exit{Feste}

\stage{Re-enter \character{Malvolio}}

\speech[Malvolio]{Madam, yond young fellow swears he will speak with you. I told him you were sick; he takes on him to understand so much, and therefore comes to speak with you. I told him you were asleep; he seems to have a foreknowledge of that too, and therefore comes to speak with you. What is to be said to him, lady? he's fortified against any denial.}

\speech[Olivia]{Tell him he shall not speak with me.}

\speech[Malvolio]{Has been told so; and he says, he'll stand at your door like a sheriff's post, and be the supporter to a bench, but he'll speak with you.}

\speech[Olivia]{What kind o' man is he?}

\speech[Malvolio]{Why, of mankind.}

\speech[Olivia]{What manner of man?}

\speech[Malvolio]{Of very ill manner; he'll speak with you, will you or no.}

\speech[Olivia]{Of what personage and years is he?}

\speech[Malvolio]{Not yet old enough for a man, nor young enough for a boy; as a squash is before 'tis a peascod, or a cooling when 'tis almost an apple: 'tis with him in standing water, between boy and man. He is very well-favoured and he speaks very shrewishly; one would think his mother's milk were scarce out of him.}

\speech[Olivia]{Let him approach: call in my gentlewoman.}

\speech[Malvolio]{Gentlewoman, my lady calls.}
\exit{Malvolio}

\stage{Re-enter \character{Maria}}

\speech[Olivia]{Give me my veil: come, throw it o'er my face. We'll once more hear Orsino's embassy.}

\enter{Viola, Attendants}

\speech[Viola]{The honourable lady of the house, which is she?}

\speech[Olivia]{Speak to me; I shall answer for her. Your will?}

\speech[Viola]{Most radiant, exquisite and unmatchable beauty,—I pray you, tell me if this be the lady of the house, for I never saw her: I would be loath to cast away my speech, for besides that it is excellently well penned, I have taken great pains to con it. Good beauties, let me sustain no scorn; I am very comptible, even to the least sinister usage.}

\speech[Olivia]{Whence came you, sir?}

\speech[Viola]{I can say little more than I have studied, and that question's out of my part. Good gentle one, give me modest assurance if you be the lady of the house, that I may proceed in my speech.}

\speech[Olivia]{Are you a comedian?}

\speech[Viola]{No, my profound heart: and yet, by the very fangs of malice I swear, I am not that I play. Are you the lady of the house?}

\speech[Olivia]{If I do not usurp myself, I am.}

\speech[Viola]{Most certain, if you are she, you do usurp yourself; for what is yours to bestow is not yours to reserve. But this is from my commission: I will on with my speech in your praise, and then show you the heart of my message.}

\speech[Olivia]{Come to what is important in't: I forgive you the praise.}

\speech[Viola]{Alas, I took great pains to study it, and 'tis poetical.}

\speech[Olivia]{It is the more like to be feigned: I pray you, keep it in. I heard you were saucy at my gates, and allowed your approach rather to wonder at you than to hear you. If you be not mad, be gone; if you have reason, be brief: 'tis not that time of moon with me to make one in so skipping a dialogue.}

\speech[Maria]{Will you hoist sail, sir? here lies your way.}

\speech[Viola]{No, good swabber; I am to hull here a little longer. Some mollification for your giant, sweet lady. Tell me your mind: I am a messenger.}

\speech[Olivia]{Sure, you have some hideous matter to deliver, when the courtesy of it is so fearful. Speak your office.}

\speech[Viola]{It alone concerns your ear. I bring no overture of war, no taxation of homage: I hold the olive in my hand; my words are as full of peace as matter.}

\speech[Olivia]{Yet you began rudely. What are you? what would you?}

\speech[Viola]{The rudeness that hath appeared in me have I learned from my entertainment. What I am, and what I would, are as secret as maidenhead; to your ears, divinity, to any other's, profanation.}

\speech[Olivia]{Give us the place alone: we will hear this divinity.

\exit{Maria, Attendants}

Now, sir, what is your text?}

\speech[Viola]{Most sweet lady,—}

\speech[Olivia]{A comfortable doctrine, and much may be said of it. Where lies your text?}

\speech[Viola]{In Orsino's bosom.}

\speech[Olivia]{In his bosom! In what chapter of his bosom?}

\speech[Viola]{To answer by the method, in the first of his heart.}

\speech[Olivia]{O, I have read it: it is heresy. Have you no more to say?}

\speech[Viola]{Good madam, let me see your face.}

\speech[Olivia]{Have you any commission from your lord to negotiate with my face? You are now out of your text: but we will draw the curtain and show you the picture. Look you, sir, such a one I was this present: is't not well done?}

\speech[Viola]{Excellently done, if God did all.}

\speech[Olivia]{'Tis in grain, sir; 'twill endure wind and weather.}

\speech[Viola]{'Tis beauty truly blent, whose red and white\\
Nature's own sweet and cunning hand laid on:\\
Lady, you are the cruell'st she alive,\\
If you will lead these graces to the grave\\
And leave the world no copy.}

\speech[Olivia]{O, sir, I will not be so hard-hearted; I will give out divers schedules of my beauty: it shall be inventoried, and every particle and utensil labelled to my will: as, item, two lips, indifferent red; item, two grey eyes, with lids to them; item, one neck, one chin, and so forth. Were you sent hither to praise me?}

\speech[Viola]{I see you what you are, you are too proud;\\
But, if you were the devil, you are fair.\\
My lord and master loves you: O, such love\\
Could be but recompensed, though you were crown'd\\
The nonpareil of beauty!}


\speech[Olivia]{How does he love me?}


\speech[Viola]{With adorations, fertile tears,\\
With groans that thunder love, with sighs of fire.}


\speech[Olivia]{Your lord does know my mind; I cannot love him:\\
Yet I suppose him virtuous, know him noble,\\
Of great estate, of fresh and stainless youth;\\
In voices well divulged, free, learn'd and valiant;\\
And in dimension and the shape of nature\\
A gracious person: but yet I cannot love him;\\
He might have took his answer long ago.}


\speech[Viola]{If I did love you in my master's flame,\\
With such a suffering, such a deadly life,\\
In your denial I would find no sense;\\
I would not understand it.}

\speech[Olivia]{Why, what would you?}

\speech[Viola]{Make me a willow cabin at your gate,\\
And call upon my soul within the house;\\
Write loyal cantons of contemned love\\
And sing them loud even in the dead of night;\\
Halloo your name to the reverberate hills\\
And make the babbling gossip of the air\\
Cry out <Olivia!> O, You should not rest\\
Between the elements of air and earth,\\
But you should pity me!}


\speech[Olivia]{You might do much.\\
What is your parentage?}


\speech[Viola]{Above my fortunes, yet my state is well:\\
I am a gentleman.}

\speech[Olivia]{Get you to your lord;\\
I cannot love him: let him send no more;\\
Unless, perchance, you come to me again,\\
To tell me how he takes it. Fare you well:\\
I thank you for your pains: spend this for me.}


\speech[Viola]{I am no fee'd post, lady; keep your purse:\\
My master, not myself, lacks recompense.\\
Love make his heart of flint that you shall love;\\
And let your fervor, like my master's, be\\
Placed in contempt! Farewell, fair cruelty.}

\exit{Viola}

\speech[Olivia]{<What is your parentage?>\\
<Above my fortunes, yet my state is well:\\
I am a gentleman.> I'll be sworn thou art;\\
Thy tongue, thy face, thy limbs, actions and spirit,\\
Do give thee five-fold blazon: not too fast:\\
soft, soft!\\
Unless the master were the man. How now!\\
Even so quickly may one catch the plague?\\
Methinks I feel this youth's perfections\\
With an invisible and subtle stealth\\
To creep in at mine eyes. Well, let it be.\\
What ho, Malvolio!}

\stage{Re-enter \character{Malvolio}}

\speech[Malvolio]{Here, madam, at your service.}

\speech[Olivia]{Run after that same peevish messenger,\\
The county's man: he left this ring behind him,\\
Would I or not: tell him I'll none of it.\\
Desire him not to flatter with his lord,\\
Nor hold him up with hopes; I am not for him:\\
If that the youth will come this way to-morrow,\\
I'll give him reasons for't: hie thee, Malvolio.}


\speech[Malvolio]{Madam, I will.}
\exit{Malvolio}

\speech[Olivia]{I do I know not what, and fear to find\\
Mine eye too great a flatterer for my mind.\\
Fate, show thy force: ourselves we do not owe;\\
What is decreed must be, and be this so.}

\exit{Olivia}
