\Scene{3}[\character{Olivia's} house]

\enter{Sir Toby Belch, Maria}

\speech[Toby]{What a plague means my niece, to take the death of her brother thus? I am sure care's an enemy to life.}

\speech[Maria]{By my troth, Sir Toby, you must come in earlier o' nights: your cousin, my lady, takes great exceptions to your ill hours.}

\speech[Toby]{Why, let her except, before excepted.}

\speech[Maria]{Ay, but you must confine yourself within the modest limits of order.}

\speech[Toby]{Confine! I'll confine myself no finer than I am: these clothes are good enough to drink in; and so be these boots too: an they be not, let them hang themselves in their own straps.}

\speech[Maria]{That quaffing and drinking will undo you: I heard my lady talk of it yesterday; and of a foolish knight that you brought in one night here to be her wooer.}

\speech[Toby]{Who, Sir Andrew Aguecheek?}

\speech[Maria]{Ay, he.}

\speech[Toby]{He's as tall a man as any's in Illyria.}

\speech[Maria]{What's that to the purpose?}

\speech[Toby]{Why, he has three thousand ducats a year.}

\speech[Maria]{Ay, but he'll have but a year in all these ducats: he's a very fool and a prodigal.}

\speech[Toby]{Fie, that you'll say so! he plays o' the viol-de-gamboys, and speaks three or four languages word for word without book, and hath all the good gifts of nature.}

\speech[Maria]{He hath indeed, almost natural: for besides that he's a fool, he's a great quarreller: and but that he hath the gift of a coward to allay the gust he hath in quarrelling, 'tis thought among the prudent he would quickly have the gift of a grave.}

\speech[Toby]{By this hand, they are scoundrels and subtractors that say so of him. Who are they?}

\speech[Maria]{They that add, moreover, he's drunk nightly in your company.}

\speech[Toby]{With drinking healths to my niece: I'll drink to her as long as there is a passage in my throat and drink in Illyria: he's a coward and a coystrill that will not drink to my niece till his brains turn o' the toe like a parish-top. What, wench! Castiliano vulgo! for here comes Sir Andrew Agueface.}

\enter{Sir Andrew Aguecheek}

\speech[Andrew]{Sir Toby Belch! how now, Sir Toby Belch!}

\speech[Toby]{Sweet Sir Andrew!}

\speech[Andrew]{Bless you, fair shrew.}

\speech[Maria]{And you too, sir.}

\speech[Toby]{Accost, Sir Andrew, accost.}

\speech[Andrew]{What's that?}

\speech[Toby]{My niece's chambermaid.}

\speech[Andrew]{Good Mistress Accost, I desire better acquaintance.}

\speech[Maria]{My name is Mary, sir.}

\speech[Andrew]{Good Mistress Mary Accost,—}

\speech[Toby]{You mistake, knight; <accost> is front her, board her, woo her, assail her.}

\speech[Andrew]{By my troth, I would not undertake her in this company. Is that the meaning of <accost>?}

\speech[Maria]{Fare you well, gentlemen.}

\speech[Toby]{An thou let part so, Sir Andrew, would thou mightst never draw sword again.}

\speech[Andrew]{An you part so, mistress, I would I might never draw sword again. Fair lady, do you think you have fools in hand?}

\speech[Maria]{Sir, I have not you by the hand.}

\speech[Andrew]{Marry, but you shall have; and here's my hand.}

\speech[Maria]{Now, sir, 'thought is free:' I pray you, bring your hand to the buttery-bar and let it drink.}

\speech[Andrew]{Wherefore, sweet-heart? what's your metaphor?}

\speech[Maria]{It's dry, sir.}

\speech[Andrew]{Why, I think so: I am not such an ass but I can keep my hand dry. But what's your jest?}

\speech[Maria]{A dry jest, sir.}

\speech[Andrew]{Are you full of them?}

\speech[Maria]{Ay, sir, I have them at my fingers' ends: marry, now I let go your hand, I am barren.}

\exit{Maria}

\speech[Toby]{O knight thou lackest a cup of canary: when did I see thee so put down?}

\speech[Andrew]{Never in your life, I think; unless you see canary put me down. Methinks sometimes I have no more wit than a Christian or an ordinary man has: but I am a great eater of beef and I believe that does harm to my wit.}

\speech[Toby]{No question.}

\speech[Andrew]{An I thought that, I'ld forswear it. I'll ride home to-morrow, Sir Toby.}

\speech[Toby]{Pourquoi, my dear knight?}

\speech[Andrew]{What is <Pourquoi>? do or not do? I would I had bestowed that time in the tongues that I have in fencing, dancing and bear-baiting: O, had I but followed the arts!}

\speech[Toby]{Then hadst thou had an excellent head of hair.}

\speech[Andrew]{Why, would that have mended my hair?}

\speech[Toby]{Past question; for thou seest it will not curl by nature.}

\speech[Andrew]{But it becomes me well enough, does't not?}

\speech[Toby]{Excellent; it hangs like flax on a distaff; and I hope to see a housewife take thee between her legs and spin it off.}

\speech[Andrew]{Faith, I'll home to-morrow, Sir Toby: your niece will not be seen; or if she be, it's four to one she'll none of me: the count himself here hard by woos her.}

\speech[Toby]{She'll none o' the count: she'll not match above her degree, neither in estate, years, nor wit; I have heard her swear't. Tut, there's life in't, man.}

\speech[Andrew]{I'll stay a month longer. I am a fellow o' the strangest mind i' the world; I delight in masques and revels sometimes altogether.}

\speech[Toby]{Art thou good at these kickshawses, knight?}

\speech[Andrew]{As any man in Illyria, whatsoever he be, under the degree of my betters; and yet I will not compare with an old man.}

\speech[Toby]{What is thy excellence in a galliard, knight?}

\speech[Andrew]{Faith, I can cut a caper.}

\speech[Toby]{And I can cut the mutton to't.}

\speech[Andrew]{And I think I have the back-trick simply as strong as any man in Illyria.}

\speech[Toby]{Wherefore are these things hid? wherefore have these gifts a curtain before 'em? are they like to take dust, like Mistress Mall's picture? why dost thou not go to church in a galliard and come home in a coranto? My very walk should be a jig; I would not so much as make water but in a sink-a-pace. What dost thou mean? Is it a world to hide virtues in? I did think, by the excellent constitution of thy leg, it was formed under the star of a galliard.}

\speech[Andrew]{Ay, 'tis strong, and it does indifferent well in a flame-coloured stock. Shall we set about some revels?}

\speech[Toby]{What shall we do else? were we not born under Taurus?}

\speech[Andrew]{Taurus! That's sides and heart.}

\speech[Toby]{No, sir; it is legs and thighs. Let me see the caper; ha! higher: ha, ha! excellent!}

\exit{}
